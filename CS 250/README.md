This is the collected work that I've done for the class CS 250 The software developement lifecycle, this includes my journals and final projects along with some of the codding assignmentd I worked on throughout the course. This course covered varying methods of the software developing lifestyle, focusing specifically on Agile methods and compares how Agile is different from the old industry standard of the water fall method. We were taken through a mock Agile project for the SNHU Travel company and had the opportunity to try different Agile roles to see how a Scrum team operates as a whole.

Questions:
1. How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?
   
To interpret user needs, it first helps to have information on what the user of the software is looking for to get a general idea of the direction that the tema will take to create the software. This usual involves an initial meeting with the customer and many more meetings throughout the software developement cycle to make sure the project is on track and meeting the standards of the customer. The team then takes the information and breaks it down into smaller "stories", these are just tasks for the team to work on durring sprints so the software can be incrementally created. The size of these stories is determined by the team and get updated as they work through these stories items and the project progresses to the point where the team feels they have a piece of software that is "done". 

2. How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?
 
Developing software is a team approach and it is important that everyone works together and does their part to make sure the project is completed in a timely and efficient manner. I've learned it's important to develop incremental features and slowly chip away at them until the software is finished as opposed to working until the program is done then presenting the project. I hope to take the Agile principles of flexibility, self-empowerement, and communciation with me in my future developement work as I feel these things will help not only me grow as a developer but help the company/people I work with grow as well.
 
3. What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

To be a good team member it means putting the project as the 1st priorit instead of individuals taking credit or only doing one job per person, it's about taking responsibility for the whole thing not just one section. If something goes wrong it's about finding solutions, not pointing the finger at soemone else to blame as the whole team takes responsibility for the softwares success or failure. I fell knowing ones strengths along with ones weaknesses is important along with a willing to share or grow in the corresponding areas so the whole team can benefit, this also promotes an attitude of constant learning as opposed to being stagnant in one area. Lastly, it is about communicating effectively and openly with team members so everyone is on the same page and has the information they need to complete the project.
